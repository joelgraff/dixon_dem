# Dixon High School — Historical Notes

Source for the Sierra-parser scene spine and canon layer. Compiled 2026-10-20.

## Origins

- First high school department opened in 1858 inside the old Methodist church on Second Street.
- In 1868 the district split into north and south sides, each with its own high school.
- The south side school, organized that same year, is the same institution as today's Dixon High — the lineage that carries forward.
- The north side building went up in 1869 on Ottawa and Morgan, expanded in 1900.
- The south side's white brick school burned in 1907. Students doubled up at the north school until a new South Central building opened in 1909, designed by Joseph Royer for $70,000. Reagan attended as a kid before transferring to North Dixon for his senior year in 1928.

## Consolidation and the Castle

- 1927: voters approved consolidating into one school. The board toured buildings within a hundred miles, selected Urbana firm Royer, Danely and Smith, and chose a seven-acre athletic field site along the Rock River.
- The $600,000 castle-style building opened December 14, 1929 — seven weeks after Black Thursday.
- State Superintendent Francis Blair called it a structure all passing through the city would see and marvel at.
- Wings and a new gym came in 1959; a Freshman Academy in 2007.

## Football

- 1925 team went unbeaten 7-0-1 with Neil "Moon" Reagan at end, coached by A.C. Bowers. The athletic field is named for Bowers.

## Game hooks

- Reagan's transfer from South Central to North Dixon for senior year is a small fork-worthy beat.
- The 1929 opening during the Depression is a natural crisis-state scene: a town celebrating a castle while the economy collapses.
- Walgreen's 1889 graduation sits inside the Swissville / condensed-milk era — ties the school to George Ham Page's industrial footprint.
- Crundwell's 1971 class puts the embezzlement story inside a living-memory timeframe for the 1968 Villiger scene's era.
