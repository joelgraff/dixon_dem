# Ronald Reagan Boyhood Home (+ Visitor Center / statue)

**Address:** 816 S. Hennepin Avenue (a stretch of Hennepin Ave is co-named "Reagan Way"), Dixon, IL 61021.
**Status:** NRHP-listed **March 26, 1982** (ref. #82002580). Operated as a historic house museum, currently under the Ronald Reagan Boyhood Home Foundation / Young America's Foundation.

## Location / orientation
Faces roughly east onto Hennepin Avenue, a quiet in-town residential street of similar-vintage houses set close together on narrow lots. A modern **Visitor Center and gift shop** sits immediately next door (adjacent parcel), used as the entry point for tours. A **separate** bronze statue of Reagan on horseback (9 ft tall, dedicated 2007, depicting his 1950 "Injun Summer Days" homecoming ride) stands in a **different location** — a small park where Hennepin Ave/Reagan Way meets the Rock River, not at the house itself. Keep these as two distinct set pieces.

## History
- House built **1891**.
- The Reagan family (Jack, Nelle, Neil, and young Ronald) lived here **1920–1923** — Ronald was roughly 9–12 years old.
- Restored to its 1920s appearance and furnished in period style; open to the public seasonally (April–October per some sources).

## Footprint & heights (estimated from photos)
- Classic **two-storey** Queen Anne massing on a small in-town lot (well under 1 acre total, per NRHP).
- Storey heights: fairly modest, wood-frame domestic scale, ~2.7–3 m per floor.
- Ridge height at the tallest cross-gable: **estimate 9–10 m** above grade.
- Footprint: **estimate 9 m × 12 m** main block (typical narrow-lot Queen Anne cottage), plus the wraparound porch extending ~2–2.5 m further on the front and one side.

## Massing breakdown (primitives)
1. Two-storey rectangular main volume.
2. A **cross-gable** roof — a front-facing gable intersecting a longer side ridge (classic Queen Anne massing), each gable end tall and steep.
3. A projecting bay/gable "tower-lite" element at the front corner (2-storey projecting bay under its own small gable, seen in reference photos).
4. A single-storey **wraparound porch** hugging the front and (partially) one side elevation, its own separate low hip/shed roof.
5. Tall slender brick chimney rising through the main roof ridge.

## Facade description (bay by bay)
- **Siding:** narrow white-painted wood lap/clapboard siding on the main wall planes.
- **Gable ends:** decorative **fish-scale and diamond-pattern cut shingles** (a contrasting texture band distinguishing the gable peaks from the clapboard below) — small **diamond-shaped louvered attic vents** centered in the gable peaks.
- **Windows:** narrow 1-over-1 double-hung sash, plain white surrounds, lace curtains visible inside; window placement is asymmetric/picturesque per Queen Anne convention (paired and single windows mixed).
- **Porch:** single-storey, wraps the front and turns the corner along one side; **turned/tapered square Tuscan-style wood columns**, simple white balustrade railing, wood lattice skirting (white-painted) closing the gap beneath the porch deck.
- **Foundation:** rough coursed **tan-buff limestone/rubble** foundation visible at grade beneath the clapboard, most visible where the porch skirting doesn't cover it.
- **Entry:** front door under the porch roof, address numerals **"816"** mounted above/beside the door; a bronze/metal historical marker sign stands near the front walk.

## Materials & real colors (hex estimates)
- Clapboard siding: white, `#F1EFE8` (sunlit) to `#D6D3C8` (shadow) — a soft warm white, not stark/blue-white.
- Roof shingles: weathered gray-brown composition/asphalt shingle, `#8A8378` — note the house was originally cedar-shingled; today's roof reads as a gray-brown asphalt shingle in photos.
- Foundation stone: tan-buff limestone, `#C7BC9E`.
- Porch lattice/trim: white, matching siding.
- Chimney: red brick, `#8A4A3A`.
- Door: dark stained wood/painted dark green or brown, `#2E2620` (estimate, not clearly visible).

## Roof
Steep **cross-gabled** roof, composition shingle, gray-brown, moderate pitch (~40–45°), with a tall brick chimney piercing the main ridge and small diamond vents in each gable peak.

## Signage
Small brass/bronze NRHP-style historical marker on a post near the front walk (text not legible at distance — low-res decal is fine).

## Notable details worth modelling
- Wraparound porch with turned columns and white lattice skirt — a strong, easily-read Queen Anne signature.
- Decorative fish-scale/diamond shingle bands in the gable peaks, contrasting texture against the plain clapboard.
- Diamond-shaped attic vents.
- Exposed rubble-stone foundation course.
- Similar-vintage neighbor houses crowd close on both sides — do not model this house in isolation; it reads as part of a tight residential streetscape.

## Surrounding context
Narrow in-town residential lot, mature shade trees, small front lawn, concrete sidewalk close to the porch steps, on-street parking, a modern Visitor Center building immediately adjacent (simpler, non-historic massing — keep it visually subordinate to the house). The Reagan-on-horseback statue and its riverside park are a separate, distant set piece near the Hennepin Ave/Rock River junction — do not conflate the two locations.

## Must-get-right (top 5 for recognizability)
1. **White clapboard** Queen Anne with a **steep cross-gable roof**.
2. **Wraparound porch** with turned/tapered columns and white lattice skirting.
3. Tall brick chimney through the main ridge.
4. **Diamond and fish-scale shingle detailing** in the gable peaks with diamond attic vents.
5. Visible tan-buff **rubble stone foundation** course at grade, and tight, similar-vintage neighbor houses close on both sides.
