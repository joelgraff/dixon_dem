# Galena Avenue Bridge (over the Rock River)

**Location:** Carries **Galena Avenue / U.S. Route 52 (also IL Route 2)** across the Rock River in downtown Dixon, connecting the courthouse/downtown block on the south bank to North Dixon.
**Current structure:** rebuilt **2002–2003** — a total replacement including new piers (per contemporaneous local documentation of the construction). Wikimedia Commons categorizes it as **"Galena Avenue Bridge (Dixon, Illinois, 2002)."**

## IMPORTANT naming note
The brief calls this the **"Truman Memorial Bridge."** I could not verify that name in any source (NRHP, IDOT, Wikipedia, local news, or historic-bridge databases) after extensive searching. Every source — local walking-tour pages, Wikimedia Commons, IDOT project pages, and news coverage — refers to it simply as the **"Galena Avenue Bridge."** Treat "Truman Memorial Bridge" as **unconfirmed/likely mistaken**; model and label it as the Galena Avenue Bridge unless the user can supply a source. (Do not confuse with the 1869 **Truesdell Bridge**, a wholly different iron truss bridge on this same site that collapsed catastrophically in 1873, killing 46 — a notable piece of Dixon history but not the structure standing today.)

## Location / orientation
Runs north–south, spanning the Rock River at the point where the river bends through downtown. Approached from the south by the courthouse/Nachusa House block of Galena Ave, from the north into North Dixon's commercial strip. The dam/powerhouse is a short distance **upstream (west)** of the bridge.

## Dimensions (estimated from photos — pier count, deck height, comparison to riverside buildings)
- **Roadway width:** wide urban arterial — 4 travel lanes plus a raised sidewalk each side, plus decorative string-light poles mounted on the rail. **Estimate 22–25 m (72–82 ft)** curb to curb including sidewalks.
- **Total span/length:** river is broad here (millpond-width above the dam); counting visible piers in reference photos (7–8 pier bents), **estimate 150–180 m (500–600 ft)** total length.
- **Deck height above normal water:** modest, low-clearance urban bridge — **estimate 4–5 m (13–16 ft)**.
- **Span length between piers:** fairly short/regular, **estimate 18–22 m (60–70 ft)** each.

## Structure type & massing
A **continuous concrete haunched (voided) slab / girder bridge** — NOT a truss, NOT steel girders visibly exposed. Key visual signature: at each pier, the underside of the deck **flares/splays outward and downward** in a fan/haunch shape before meeting the pier cap — giving a distinctive scalloped rhythm when viewed from the riverbank or from below. Pier caps have rounded (bullnose) ends facing up- and down-stream, sitting on rectangular pier shafts rising from the water/riverbed.

Primitives: long low rectangular deck slab (with a subtle upward camber/arc when viewed in profile from the bank) + repeating haunched-pier-cap units (a tapered wedge/fan shape) + rectangular pier shafts.

## Facade / details
- **Railing:** simple painted steel tube/pipe railing (2–3 horizontal rails) along both sidewalks — utilitarian, not ornamental.
- **Sidewalk:** the walkway includes the words **"Lincoln Highway"** formed/stamped into the concrete (a nod to the historic Lincoln Highway auto route that once ran through Dixon on this alignment) — a nice low-poly decal/texture detail.
- **Lighting:** ornate black cobra-head or acorn-style streetlights along the bridge, plus overhead string-light cabling visible in some photos (seasonal decoration, optional).
- **Piers:** cream/light-gray concrete, algae/waterline staining near the base (darker gray-green band at the waterline).

## Materials & colors (hex estimates)
- Deck/parapet concrete: light gray-buff, `#C9C4B8` (dry, sunlit) to `#A9A79C` (wet/shadow).
- Pier waterline staining: dark gray-green `#5C625A`.
- Railings: painted steel, weathered silver-gray `#8C8C86`.
- River water: grey-green Rock River water, `#7C8B82` under overcast, darker `#4B5A55` in shadow/under the bridge.

## Signage

## Notable details worth modelling
- The **fan/haunch pier detail** is the single most distinctive silhouette element — worth getting right even at low poly (a simple flare on the underside geometry reads correctly from the riverbank, which is a likely player vantage).
- "Lincoln Highway" text stamped in the sidewalk.
- Downtown skyline backdrop visible from the bridge: 5–6 storey former factory/warehouse and apartment buildings on the southwest bank (cream/tan brick, flat roofs, small penthouse/tower features), useful as a background silhouette when standing on or near the bridge.
- Orange safety buoys strung across the river just upstream, marking the dam hazard line (see `dixon_dam.md`).

## Surrounding context
Downtown brick buildings crowd the south/west bank right up to the water; the north bank is more open/industrial. Riverbank has mown grass, some mature trees, and a paved riverfront trail/promenade in the Heritage Crossing area near the bridge. Mast-arm traffic signals at both bridge approaches.

## Must-get-right (top 5 for recognizability)
1. Long, low, cream-concrete **haunched-slab bridge** with the fan-shaped pier haunches visible from below/the bank.
2. Multiple evenly spaced river piers with rounded (bullnose) caps.
3. Simple steel pipe/tube railings, not ornamental trusswork.
4. **"Lincoln Highway"** text cast into the sidewalk.
5. Positioned with the dense downtown brick-building skyline immediately on its south/west end and the dam just upstream.

## Corrections from on-site photos (Nov 2025, user-supplied; supersede the estimates above)
The structure-type estimate above was made from low-resolution photos and is **wrong in several respects**. From the Nov 2025 BHRC street-level photos:
- **Superstructure is steel plate girders, visible from the bank**: a pale blue-gray steel girder line under a buff concrete deck fascia (~0.6 m deep), with bolted splice plates and bearing stiffeners. Girder depth is greatest at the piers and shallows toward midspan (haunched profile), so the soffit reads as a gentle scallop between piers. It is not a concrete slab.
- **Piers are fat concrete columns with rounded (bullnose) ends**, 2-3 columns per bent seen in the river, wide-spaced; light gray with waterline staining. Pier caps are hidden by the girders.
- **Parapet**: continuous concrete barrier at the deck edge with a **dark green (teal-black) ornamental steel railing** on top -- vertical pickets, round medallion/ring panel between top and bottom rails, posts at intervals. Not silver pipe.
- **Roadway/sidewalk separation**: a concrete safety barrier with a single dark green steel rail on top separates the traffic lanes from the raised sidewalks on both sides. The sidewalk is about 2.5-3 m wide.
- **Lights**: double-globe ornamental poles (white globes, dark green/black pole with a curved bracket), set at the sidewalk edge at roughly 25-30 m spacing, outside the railing line.
- **Deck level**: the deck is essentially level with the street grade at both ends; the west bank has tall limestone-faced retaining walls and abutment wing walls rather than an embankment ramp.
- Pavement is light gray concrete with white lane lines.

Additional Jun 2026 Street View photos (`user_2026_*.webp`: both approaches, and midspan looking each way):
- **Deck is level with the street at both approaches** and carries on at the same grade; the whole structure is a flat, low crossing (soffit ~3 m over the pool, so deck ~4.5 m above pool -- pipeline uses 4.5 m, not the old 7.5 m).
- **Roadway is light-gray concrete** (not asphalt) with transverse joints and a few cracks; markings are a **double yellow centreline**, white dashed lane lines, solid white edge lines, and large white outlined **"Lincoln Hwy" letters painted in the lane** (reading along the lane, ~6 m long, with a "+" mark and turn arrows nearby), not a sidewalk stamp. A thin dark seam line crosses the deck at the pier/expansion joint.
- Midspan view: a **concrete barrier ~1 m high** runs along each edge directly beside the outer lane with the green railing on top (sidewalk lies outside it on the downstream side near the ends; elsewhere the barrier is the edge).
- **Approach (south end)**: signalised intersection at the bridge foot with mast-arm signals (four heads per arm), curbed shoulders, brick-paver corner pads, hanging flower baskets on the ornate lamps, and 2-3 storey Italianate brick storefronts with striped awnings immediately along both sides.
