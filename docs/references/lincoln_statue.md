# Lincoln Statue (Lincoln Monument State Memorial / Fort Dixon site)

**Location:** Lincoln Monument State Memorial, on the **west side of N. Galena Avenue** (US 52 / IL 26), **north of the Rock River**, at/near "Lincoln Statue Drive" — marking the site of the 1832 **Fort Dixon / Dixon Blockhouse**, a Black Hawk War military post. Maintained as an Illinois state historic site by the Illinois Historic Preservation Division.

## History
- Site significance: Fort Dixon was officially established here May 22, 1832, during the Black Hawk War. Abraham Lincoln (then a 23-year-old volunteer militia captain), Zachary Taylor, and Jefferson Davis were all present in the area during the campaign; Lincoln's company camped roughly a mile west on May 12, 1832.
- **Statue:** bronze, sculpted by **Leonard Crunelle**, dedicated **September 24, 1930**.
- Depicts a **beardless, youthful Lincoln** as he looked as a militia captain in 1832 — not the familiar bearded president.
- Rear of the granite base carries an inscription commemorating Dixon's founder, **John Dixon**.

## Statue dimensions
Bronze figure: **300 cm × 61 cm × 46 cm** (~10 ft tall × 2 ft × 1.5 ft) — a larger-than-life standing figure. Add the pedestal for total monument height — **estimate 5–6 m (16–20 ft)** overall including the stepped granite base, based on photo proportions against surrounding mature trees.

## Massing breakdown (primitives)
1. Standing bronze human figure (full-length, static "at ease" pose) — a single sculptural mesh, doesn't need primitive breakdown, but proportion it as an adult male ~2× normal height scale relative to the pedestal (larger-than-life, not exactly life-size).
2. A tall, roughly rectangular **granite die/pedestal block**, slightly tapered or battered sides, with "LINCOLN" incised in raised or engraved lettering on the front face.
3. A stepped granite base (2–3 steps) beneath the die, widening toward the ground.
4. Low plinth/foundation slab flush with the surrounding sidewalk.

## Pose / details
- Lincoln stands in a long frock coat / greatcoat, waistcoat and trousers, looking naturalistic rather than heroic-idealized.
- One hand holds his **hat** down at his side; the other hand rests near his belt, close to what appears to be a walking stick or rifle butt (period militia captain attire) — model this as a simple vertical prop object at the figure's side if going to prop-level detail.
- Weathered bronze surface: strong **verdigris green-teal patina** over most of the figure with darker bronze-brown showing through at high-touch/high points (shoulders, hat, boot tops) — classic outdoor bronze weathering.

## Materials & colors (hex estimates)
- Bronze body (weathered): teal-green patina `#5F8A78` to `#7DA692` on broad surfaces, with darker warm bronze-brown `#4A3C2E` showing at edges/high points and streak-staining `#3D5449` in recesses/folds.
- Granite pedestal: cool gray, `#8B8C86` (sunlit) to `#6B6C67` (shadow), fairly uniform, minimal veining.
- Base/steps: matching gray granite, same palette.

## Signage
"LINCOLN" carved into the pedestal face (front); a dedication/historical inscription on the rear face referencing John Dixon (small, not legible at a distance — can be a low-res decal).

## Notable details worth modelling
- The **weathered bronze patina** is the single most important shading detail — should not read as shiny new bronze; use a worn/matte teal-green material with warm highlights breaking through at edges.
- Stepped granite base — good excuse for simple, readable geometric steps at the monument's foot, useful for player scale reference.
- Surrounding wood picket fence backdrop visible in reference photo (site boundary/park edge).

## Surrounding context
Small, quiet landscaped park/memorial site — mature deciduous trees (bare in the reference photo, so include both leafed and bare-winter variants), grass, a simple concrete walkway leading to the monument, low wood picket fence marking a property line nearby, ordinary commercial buildings and a gas-station-style sign visible just beyond the site in the background (this is essentially a small pocket park along a busy arterial, not an isolated monument ground) — keep the immediate setting modest and slightly informal rather than grand/plaza-like.

## Must-get-right (top 5 for recognizability)
1. **Young, beardless Lincoln** in a frock coat, holding his hat — not the bearded presidential Lincoln.
2. Strong **weathered teal-green bronze patina**, not shiny/new-looking.
3. Tall **gray granite pedestal** with "LINCOLN" carved on the front face, stepped base.
4. Set within a small, tree-shaded pocket park directly beside a busy street (N. Galena Ave), not a grand isolated plaza.
5. Location north of the river, near the Fort Dixon/blockhouse historic site — distinct from the boyhood-home Reagan-on-horseback statue elsewhere in town.
