# Nachusa House

**Address:** 215 S. Galena Avenue, Dixon, IL 61021 (one Wikipedia mirror lists "5 S. Galena Ave" — treat 215 S. Galena Ave as authoritative per the historical marker and most sources).
**Status:** NRHP-listed **February 10, 1983** (ref. #83000323), contributing property to the Dixon Downtown Historic District.

## Location / orientation
Faces **east onto Galena Avenue**, mid-block/corner in the downtown commercial core, a short walk from the courthouse and the Galena Ave bridge. Ground floor today has residential-building entries (converted to senior housing) rather than active hotel storefronts.

## History
- Chartered by the Dixon Hotel Company; foundation laid **1837–38**, construction stalled by the Panic of 1837.
- **Built and opened 1853** as a 4-storey limestone building (walls 2 ft / 0.6 m thick), limestone laid on the 1838 foundation.
- **West annex added 1854.**
- **1867–68: major enlargement** — added a rough-stone addition, a new top storey, and the **mansard roof** that gives the building its present iconic silhouette (this is the profile visible today).
- Stucco coating applied over the masonry in the 1880s (explains today's smooth stucco-like wall finish rather than exposed stone/brick).
- **South annex added 1915.**
- Operated continuously as a hotel until **1988**; nearly demolished in 1997; restored **1997–98** ($3.2M) into **35 apartment units** for senior citizens by the Illinois Housing Development Authority and a Chicago developer.
- Notable historic guests: Abraham Lincoln, Ulysses S. Grant (per local history).
- Name origin: "Na-Chu-Sa," said to be the Native American name for founder John Dixon, meaning roughly "head/hair of white."

## Footprint & heights (estimated from photos)
- **5 storeys** total: 4 main floors + the mansard-roof level (which itself reads as a full occupied storey with dormered windows).
- Storey height: generous 19th-century hotel scale, ~3.5–4 m for the lower floors.
- Cornice/eave line (base of mansard): **estimate 16–18 m** above grade.
- Ridge/ridge-cap height at the top of the mansard: **estimate 21–23 m** above grade — a genuinely tall building for downtown Dixon, taller than most neighboring 2–3 storey commercial blocks.
- Footprint: roughly square main block, **estimate 25 m × 25 m**, plus the deeper south (1915) and west (1854) annexes behind — model the street-facing Galena Ave block as the primary asset and simplify the annexes as lower-detail rear massing.

## Massing breakdown (primitives)
1. Large rectangular 4-storey box (main block), stucco-clad.
2. A prominent **mansard roof** (concave/curb-style profile — steep lower slope, flatter near the ridge) wrapping the top of the building, clad in dark slate-gray shingle.
3. A regular row of **pedimented dormer windows** punched through the mansard on the street-facing side — each dormer has its own small triangular/segmental-arch pediment.
4. A **two-tier porch/balcony** structure applied to the ground and 2nd floor along the full street frontage — essentially a stacked colonnade of slender posts supporting a lower porch roof and an upper open balcony with a balustrade.
5. Deep **bracketed cornice** band at the eave line, separating the flat-walled stucco stories below from the mansard above.

## Facade description (bay by bay, Galena Ave front)
- **Ground level:** painted gray brick base/water-table, modern storefront-style entries (post-restoration), simple awning-free.
- **1st–2nd floor porch zone:** a two-tier open porch spans the full width — ground floor porch with slender square posts (painted maroon/burgundy with white trim bands) supporting a floor/roof deck; the level above is an open balcony with a white painted wood balustrade railing and matching maroon-white posts, sheltered under the main porch roof.
- **3rd–4th floor (stucco wall):** plain **mustard/ochre-gold stucco** wall punctuated by regularly spaced tall double-hung windows with white surrounds — roughly 6–7 window bays visible across the photographed elevation.
- **Bracketed cornice:** dark wood brackets in a regular rhythm under the eave, painted dark brown/near-black against the white trim band.
- **Mansard level:** dark gray-brown slate-pattern shingle roof plane studded with **5+ segmental-arch pedimented dormers**, each with white surrounds/keystone trim and a small triangular or round-arch pediment cap — this dormer row is the building's most distinctive feature.

## Materials & real colors (hex estimates)
- Main wall stucco: mustard/ochre-gold, `#B79A4A` (sunlit) to `#8F7735` (shadow).
- Mansard roof cladding: dark slate-brown, `#3E332A`.
- Porch posts/trim: maroon-burgundy `#5C2A2E` with white banding `#EDE9DD`.
- Cornice brackets: near-black dark brown, `#241E18`.
- Ground-floor base: painted gray brick, `#5A5A56`.
- Window surrounds: white, `#EDE9DD`.

## Roof
**Mansard roof** — the defining feature. Concave profile, dark slate-gray/brown shingle, punctuated by a regular row of pedimented dormers on the street elevation. Flat deck roof above the mansard (not visible from the street).

## Signage
No large signage visible in current photos (residential building today); historical marker plaque near the entrance (small, low-res decal sufficient).

## Notable details worth modelling
- The **row of pedimented dormers** along the mansard — the single most recognizable feature; worth getting the rhythm and pediment shape right even if simplified.
- Two-tier porch/balcony with maroon-and-white posts — strong color accent against the ochre stucco.
- Bracketed cornice line separating wall from roof.
- Painted gray-brick base at street level.

## Surrounding context
Downtown Galena Ave streetscape: attached 2–4 storey brick commercial buildings on either side (much lower than Nachusa House, making it a clear vertical landmark on the street), street trees, ornate lamp post at the curb, on-street parking, concrete sidewalk.

## Must-get-right (top 5 for recognizability)
1. **5-storey mustard/ochre stucco mass** — noticeably taller than every neighboring downtown building.
2. Dark **mansard roof** with a regular row of pedimented dormer windows.
3. **Two-tier porch/balcony** across the full Galena Ave frontage with maroon-and-white posts.
4. Deep bracketed cornice band at the eave line.
5. Corner/mid-block position on Galena Ave near the courthouse and bridge, visibly taller than surrounding storefronts.
