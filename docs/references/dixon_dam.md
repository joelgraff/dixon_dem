# Dixon Dam (Rock River Hydroelectric Dam)

**Location:** Rock River, immediately **upstream (west) of the Galena Avenue Bridge**, downtown Dixon. FERC Project No. 2373 ("Rockton and Dixon"), currently operated by Eagle Creek Renewable Energy / STS Hydropower.

## Caveat on sourcing
I could not find an openly-licensed close-up photograph of the dam/spillway/powerhouse specifically (searched Wikimedia Commons categories for Illinois dams, Rock River, and Dixon directly — none exist as of this research). The three reference photos saved here (`img/dixon_dam/`) are wide river/skyline shots that **show the dam's location and general context** (spillway hazard buoy line, brick riverside industrial buildings) but not a clean elevation view. Physical dimensions below are from the FERC relicensing documents and historical sources; visual massing/coloring is an **estimate** from the industrial-brick-powerhouse convention of 1920s hydro plants plus what's visible in the context photos — flag this to the builder as lower-confidence than the other docs.

## History
- 1845: first (5 ft high) wood-and-stone timber-crib dam, built by the chartered Dixon Dam and Bridge Company (toll bridge + dam).
- 1904: new timber-crib dam built just downstream/adjoining the old one — rock-filled timber cribbing, 1/4" steel crest cap, 3" oak apron; powered the Dixon Hydraulic Company (~2,000 hp).
- **1925: current concrete dam built**, along with the still-standing **brick powerhouse**. This 1925 structure (refurbished over the decades) is what exists today.

## Structure & dimensions
Three functional sections (per FERC license documents):
1. **Power canal headworks** — the intake structure diverting river flow into a **1,600 ft (~490 m) long power canal** leading to the powerhouse turbines.
2. **Concrete overflow (spillway) dam** — **approximately 6 ft (~1.8 m) high**, spanning the main river channel; a low-head "run-of-river" dam, not a tall impoundment structure. Water sheets over the full crest in a continuous white curtain.
3. **Earthen dike** — closes the gap on one bank between the spillway and higher ground.

**Powerhouse:** brick industrial building, built 1925, houses **5 IP Morris vertical turbine-generator units**, rated **3,000 kW total**, producing ~13 million kWh/year. Typical 1920s hydro-powerhouse form: a rectangular brick block with tall multi-pane industrial windows, a low-pitched or flat roof, likely with a small crane gantry or roof monitor — **model as a generic 1920s brick power station** (single or double-height rectangular volume, segmental-arched or flat-headed tall windows in a regular rhythm, corbelled brick cornice) since no confirmed photo of this specific building's fenestration was found.

## Footprint (estimates)
- Spillway/dam crest length across the river: **estimate 90–120 m**, spanning the visible width of the Rock River at this point (river is broad/lake-like above the dam).
- Powerhouse footprint: **estimate 20 m × 12 m**, single or double bay.
- Height of dam above tailwater (downstream face): **estimate 2–2.5 m** given the ~6 ft crest height.

## Massing breakdown (primitives)
1. A long, low, straight concrete weir/spillway wall spanning the channel — simple rectangular slab, water sheeting over the top in a continuous curtain (particle/shader effect, not geometry).
2. A headworks/intake structure at one end (smaller vertical concrete block with gates) leading into a canal.
3. A rectangular brick powerhouse block at the downstream end of the canal, with regular tall window bays.
4. Calm, glassy **millpond water upstream** of the dam vs. **turbulent white water downstream** — an important visual/gameplay read.

## Materials & colors (hex estimates)
- Spillway concrete: weathered gray-buff, `#9C978C`, with dark algae/mineral streaking `#4A4E42` below the waterline and on the downstream face.
- Powerhouse brick: dark red-brown industrial brick, `#7A3E33` to `#5E2E27` (older/dirtier than the decorative downtown brick — soot-darkened).
- White water at the spillway: bright foam-white `#E8EDEA` with blue-gray undertones in shadow.
- Upstream millpond: calm grey-green Rock River water, `#7C8B82`.
- Safety buoys marking the hazard zone upstream of the dam: bright orange, `#E8590C` — visible strung in a line across the channel in the reference photos, a good readable warning-marker detail.

## Roof
Powerhouse: low-pitched gable or flat roof, dark gray/black (built-up or standing-seam), estimate.

## Signage
None significant beyond standard hazard/no-boating warning signage near the buoy line (small yellow/orange placards, not legible in reference photos).

## Notable details worth modelling
- **Continuous white-water curtain** over the spillway crest — the single most important visual signature of a low-head run-of-river dam; use a scrolling/foam shader rather than complex geometry.
- **Line of orange buoys** strung across the channel upstream of the dam — cheap, highly readable prop that immediately signals "dam ahead" to a player on the water.
- Contrast between the flat, brick, blocky powerhouse and the ornate downtown buildings around it — reads as utilitarian industrial architecture.

## Surrounding context
Brick multi-storey former mill/factory and apartment buildings crowd the riverbank near the dam and powerhouse (same buildings visible behind the Galena Ave bridge — see that doc). Riverfront trail and Heritage Crossing plaza are nearby on the downtown side. The dam sits at a bend in the river, with the Galena Avenue Bridge visible immediately downstream in most vantage points from the dam.

## Must-get-right (top 5 for recognizability)
1. Long, straight, **low (~2 m) concrete spillway** with continuous sheeting white water across its full width.
2. Calm wide millpond upstream contrasted with churning white water immediately downstream.
3. A blocky **red-brown brick 1920s powerhouse** at one end of the structure, with regular tall window bays.
4. Line of **orange safety buoys** strung across the channel above the dam.
5. Positioned immediately **upstream of the Galena Avenue Bridge**, with the same downtown brick skyline as backdrop.
