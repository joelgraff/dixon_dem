# Lee County Courthouse (Old)

**Address:** 112 E. Second St., Dixon, IL 61021 — the "Courthouse Square" block bounded roughly by 2nd St, Galena Ave, and 3rd St, one block south of the Rock River / Galena Ave bridge.
**Status:** NRHP-listed individually is unconfirmed, but it is a contributing property to the Dixon Downtown Historic District (NRHP-listed March 7, 2012). Still in civic use (County Board, Clerk, Recorder).

## Location / orientation
Sits on its own landscaped block ("Courthouse Square") in the heart of downtown, one block south of Galena Ave and the river. The principal, most-ornamented facade (the one photographed here) faces roughly **north/northwest** across the square's lawn — likely the 2nd St or Galena-facing elevation; treat it as the "front" for modelling purposes. The building is fully street-visible on all sides, set back ~15–20 m from the sidewalk behind lawn.

## History
Built **1899–1901**, replacing an 1840–41 brick courthouse on the same site; cost $103,395 in gold-coin dollars of the day. Classical Revival / Beaux-Arts civic design, typical of the county-courthouse boom of that era. A marker on the lawn commemorates an 1856 Abraham Lincoln campaign speech at this site.

## Footprint & heights (estimated from photos — no NRHP PDF dimensions found; treat as estimates)
- Roughly square-to-rectangular footprint, **estimate 30 m × 45 m**.
- **3 storeys** above a raised basement/podium (visible rusticated ground floor with round-arched windows reads almost as a full 4th level).
- Storey height: generous, commercial-civic scale, ~4.5–5 m per floor.
- Cornice/parapet line: **estimate 16–18 m** above grade.
- Central dome crown (incl. lantern/finial): **estimate 28–32 m** above grade.

## Massing breakdown (primitives)
1. Large rectangular box (main 3-storey block) — buff limestone.
2. Raised rusticated podium/base wrapping the whole building (arched window openings).
3. Central projecting entrance pavilion (slightly forward of the main wall plane) carrying the giant colonnade.
4. Octagonal or square drum rising above the roofline at the center, capped by
5. A shallow **hemispherical dome** (green/verdigris) with a small lantern/finial on top.
6. Corner accents: urn-shaped finials at the parapet corners (4×, one per corner of the entrance pavilion at minimum).
7. Flanking smaller domed/cupola turrets appear at the outer corners of the photographed elevation (small conical/domed caps, subordinate to the main dome) — model as simplified cylinders + cone caps.

## Facade description (bay by bay — main photographed elevation)
- **Ground floor / podium:** smooth-to-rusticated buff limestone, punched round-arched window openings (dark sash), continuous stone belt course above.
- **Upper 2 storeys, center bay (entrance pavilion):** a giant-order colonnade of **6 fluted Ionic columns** (freestanding, not engaged) standing on the podium and rising through both upper floors to support a full classical entablature.
- **Entablature** above the columns is inscribed **"LEE COUNTY COURTHOUSE"** in raised classical lettering, topped by a dentillated cornice.
- **Wings either side of the pavilion:** flat ashlar limestone wall punctuated by tall rectangular double-hung windows (dark sash, plain stone surrounds), stacked 2 per floor per bay, roughly 3 window bays visible per wing in the reference photo.
- **Entrances:** 3 sets of double doors recessed under the colonnade at grade, reached by a wide flight of stone steps flanked by low pedestals; paired **white globe lamp posts** flank the steps.
- **Parapet:** plain entablature with modillion/dentil cornice, urn finials at the pavilion's outer corners.

## Materials & real colors (hex estimates)
- **Limestone cladding (dominant):** warm buff/cream — `#D9D0B8` (sunlit) to `#B9AE93` (shadow side).
- **Dome (weathered copper/verdigris):** `#7C9A8C` to `#8FAE9E` (streaky, slightly blue-green, not uniform — copper streaking `#5E7A6C` in recesses).
- **Window sash / doors:** near-black bronze/dark brown, `#1E1A16`.
- **Lawn:** typical Midwest turf green `#5B7B3F`.
- **Sidewalk/steps:** light gray concrete/stone `#C9C6BC`.

## Roof
Flat or low-pitched behind the parapet (not visible from grade); the **dome is the only visible roof form**, shallow hemispherical, metal-clad (originally copper, now oxidized green).

## Signage
Only the carved stone entablature lettering "LEE COUNTY COURTHOUSE" — no separate signage.

## Notable details worth modelling (low-to-mid poly)
- Fluted Ionic columns with visible capital volutes (can be low-poly with a normal/detail texture for flutes at distance).
- Dentil/modillion cornice band (can be a tiling trim strip).
- Round-arched ground-floor windows with keystones.
- Corner urn finials.
- **"The Peace Cannon"** — a small ceremonial cannon on a low base on the lawn, bronze plaque reads *"THE PEACE CANNON — NEVER FIRED IN ANGER — Purchased 1856 to salute a visit here by Abraham Lincoln"* — nice background prop.
- Paired ornate globe lamp posts flanking the entry stairs.

## Surrounding context
Open lawn "square" block with mature shade trees (oak/maple canopy visible), concrete walkways radiating to the corners, low flagpole likely present (not confirmed in photos), on ceremonial-decoration days bunting/flags are hung between columns (do not treat as permanent — omit by default). Streets on all sides are ordinary downtown two-lane streets with parallel parking and mature street trees.

## Must-get-right (top 5 for recognizability)
1. The **pale green/verdigris hemispherical dome** on a drum, centered over the entrance pavilion.
2. **6-column giant-order Ionic colonnade** on the raised podium, entablature inscribed "LEE COUNTY COURTHOUSE".
3. Warm **buff/cream limestone** cladding (not gray, not white) — rusticated base, smooth ashlar above.
4. Symmetrical 3-storey civic massing on a landscaped square block with mature shade trees.
5. Corner urn finials + paired globe lamps flanking the entry stair.
