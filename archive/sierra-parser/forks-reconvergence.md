# Forks, Reconvergence, and Load-Bearing Events

## Fork model

Wherever the player diverges from the recorded chronology, an alternate timeline comes into existence. The player plays through that branch, but at some point a reconvergence is forced — not necessarily the same point for every branch. Some historical events are played through as reconvergence anchors even when they simply do not happen in the fork, because they serve the structural purpose of reconvergence, not causal inevitability.

## Load-bearing events

An event happens not because it was the only possible outcome but because the game needs it. Closer to how real history gets written than we'd like to admit — some moments get remembered because they matter structurally.

Example: Smith taken to Missouri (fork). No Nauvoo temple, angrier succession, Brigham Young still likely takes over. By 1905 Billy Sunday still shows up — same tent, thinner crowd, different faces. The reconvergence is the revival itself.

## Reconvergence rules

- Branches split, but they meet again at fixed anchor points (e.g., 1905 revival, 1927 Lowell Park).
- No path ever strands the player.
- Scoring idea: how many canon facts survive a player's intervention before the scene breaks. Tells you where agency design is too loose or too tight.
- Forks are one-shot in v1: diverge, read the dossier, timeline snaps back or collapses into a single merged history the player carries forward. Keeps the tree from growing past depth one.
- Danger: each fork multiplies canon states to track. After two or three levels deep you're maintaining a tree of worlds. The dossier compresses each branch into readable text, but underlying data still balloons.

## Open questions

- Depth limit: one fork per playthrough, or allow chained forks with forced reconvergence?
- Does the player choose the divergence point or does the record offer them?
