# Scene Spine

Eight dated scenes forming the Sierra-parser playthrough. Fixed sequence with soft gates inside each scene. Compiled 2026-10-20.

## 1. 1830 — Ogee's Ferry

Opening. Player is a newcomer with a few dollars and a question about land. Talk to Joseph Ogee, learn the ferry's for sale. First puzzle: raise cash — hauling freight or helping a settler family across. Buy the ferry, and John Dixon arrives as an NPC with his own agenda. Reconvergence anchor: Dixon's 1836 arrival still happens regardless, but his relationship to the player shifts based on how Ogee was treated.

## 2. 1843 — McKennie's Tavern (Smith Arrest)

June 23. Missouri officers Harmon Wilson and Joseph Reynolds arrest Joseph Smith on a treason charge from the 1838 Mormon War. Locked in the tavern, refused a lawyer at first. Locals including John Dixon send for attorneys; a writ of habeas corpus follows. Lee County Sheriff Campbell arrests Reynolds and Wilson. Party leaves June 26, diverts to Nauvoo, discharged July 1 — warrant defective, case without merit. Kills Missouri's third and final extradition attempt. Reynolds and Wilson later acquitted of threatening Smith; Smith filed civil suit for false imprisonment.

Player options: side with the officers, warn Smith, or watch. Dossier after shows what changed. Fork: Smith taken to Missouri → no Nauvoo temple, no westward migration as we know it, angrier succession crisis, Brigham Young still likely takes over from persecution. Martyrdom accelerates movements. Dixon loses local Mormon families; Utah doesn't disappear.

## 3. 1856 — Lincoln's Speech

July. Speech on the courthouse lawn, stayed at the Nachusa House. Quieter scene — more about listening than doing. Parser rewards asking the right questions of the crowd.

## 4. 1873 — Truesdell Bridge Collapse

May. Crowd gathered on the bridge to watch a baptism below; north span fell without warning. 46 dead, mostly women and children in heavy dresses. Crisis state active.

Named cast:
- Christan Goble threw granddaughter Gertie (age 3) to safety before drowning. Gertie survived, stayed in Dixon, died 1944 at 74.
- Colonel Henry Noble survived, lost wife Jane. Later Dixon mayor in 1890.
- Katie Sterling, 16, tangled in iron lattice — two days to cut free.
- Lucia and Althea Hendrix, ages 4 and 6, youngest victims.
- Rescuers: Eustace Shaw (16), Edgar Patrick, John Barry (revived Clara Burr rolling her over a barrel), Dailey (credited with 16 saves).

Quest: player as rescuer on the bank. Objectives: find rope, reach waterline, pull survivors before the current takes them. Time pressure from the river — each minute, a survivor's condition worsens. Named NPCs from canon can be saved or lost. Save one and that bloodline follows forward (legacy thread).

## 5. 1885 — Bloody Gulch

Murder mystery palate cleanser. Frank Thiel, 17, Bible salesman from Elgin, killed by Joseph Mosse, French-Canadian farmhand — knife and a walnut baluster. Mosse convicted; defense lawyer Solomon Hicks Bethea believed him innocent. Mosse released from Joliet 1911 after saving a guard's life. Body found by James Penrose. John Thiel, great-nephew of the victim, still presenting at Founders Day in 2016 wearing the confiscated watch chain.

I-88's road name comes from this.

## 6. 1905 — Billy Sunday Revival

Five-week revival drew over 150,000 people. Late-game reconvergence: same tent, different world depending on every fork taken. In the Smith-taken-west fork, the town is smaller and the crowd thinner, but Sunday still shows up — his itinerary didn't depend on Smith. The ripple shows in property records and family names, not in who's under the tent. A quiet absence is its own storytelling.

Also 1905: Sousa's band played the Opera House; Clarence Darrow spoke there twice; William Jennings Bryan once.

## 7. 1920s — Interurban Era / School Consolidation

Sterling-Dixon Interurban ran 1904–1925, electric trolleys. Main terminal with car barn at First and Madison. Hourly intercity cars, 25 cents to Sterling, 90 minutes round trip. Peak 1911: ~900,000 passengers; by 1924 collapsed to ~239,000; buses replaced it.

1927: voters approved consolidating into one high school. $600,000 castle-style building opened December 14, 1929 — seven weeks after Black Thursday. State Superintendent Francis Blair: a structure all passing through the city would see and marvel at. Reagan transferred from South Central to North Dixon for senior year 1928. 1925 football team unbeaten 7-0-1, Neil "Moon" Reagan at end, coach A.C. Bowers (field named for him).

Game hook: 1929 opening during the Depression is a natural crisis-state scene — a town celebrating a castle while the economy collapses. Reagan's transfer is a small fork-worthy beat. Walgreen's c. 1889 graduation ties the school to George Ham Page's Swissville industrial footprint.

## 8. 1927 — Lowell Park

Endgame. Reagan on the guard chair, credited with saving 77 lives starting 1927. Not a puzzle so much as reflection: everything changed or not, rendered in the most peaceful scene. Win condition isn't a win — it's the final dossier, a full timeline of what Dixon became because of you.
